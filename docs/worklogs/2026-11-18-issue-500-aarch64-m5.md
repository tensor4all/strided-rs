# 2026-11-18: #500 — aarch64 Apple M5 parity for the MACE segpoly kernels

Issue: [tensor4all/strided-rs#500](https://github.com/tensor4all/strided-rs/issues/500).
This is the aarch64 counterpart of #497 ([`2026-11-17-issue-496-segpoly-simd-reduce.md`](2026-11-17-issue-496-segpoly-simd-reduce.md))
and #499 ([`2026-11-18-issue-498-segpoly-fused-vjp.md`](2026-11-18-issue-498-segpoly-fused-vjp.md)),
measured with the #493 harness ([`benchmarks/strided-491-mace-ab`](../../benchmarks/strided-491-mace-ab))
on an Apple M5 Max. The library change is one loop restructuring in
`strided-segpoly/src/simd.rs`; the rest of the diff is the measurement and the
harness portability it needed.

Two aarch64 protocol differences are stated rather than papered over:

* **No CPU affinity.** macOS has no per-thread core binding and no `taskset`.
  `campaign.sh` now auto-detects this (`PIN=0`) and runs the cells unpinned; the
  1T/4T thread counts still bound each side, but a 4T cell can be scheduled on
  6 super cores or on performance cores from sample to sample. That is the main
  source of the 4T absolute spread below; the ratio is stable across repeats
  (§5.3).
* **`SETTLE=5` before every cell and anchor.** A 1024-atom VJP cell leaves about
  1.5 GiB being reclaimed. The three campaigns that ignored the pause
  (`raw/500-discarded/500-after`, `500-before2`, `500-before3`) failed their own
  15 % anchor guard with the end anchor 19.8-26.3 % slow (13.7-18.0 ms against
  11.0-13.5 ms at the start). With `SETTLE=5` the accepted pair's anchors moved
  1.3 % and 1.7-2.0 %. The pause is a protocol change, not a kernel result.
  Linux did not show the effect, so the default stays 0 and the M5 runs set
  `SETTLE=5`.

## 3. Why aarch64 differs from the evo2 x86-64 numbers

The forward ratios are *better* on M5 than on evo2 (0.236-0.391 after, against
0.215-0.457 on x86), so the difference is VJP-specific. Same four cells, before
this branch and after, ratio = strided / llmlip:

| cell | M5 before | x86 before (#499) | M5 after | x86 after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| f32 4T 128 | 0.775 | 0.741 | **0.672** | 0.570 |
| f32 4T 1024 | 0.786 | 0.520 | **0.656** | 0.351 |
| f64 4T 128 | 0.891 | 0.448 | **0.718** | 0.339 |
| f64 4T 1024 | 0.885 | 0.500 | **0.732** | 0.381 |

The absolute times say this is not the strided kernel getting slower: at f32 4T
1024 M5's tuned 49.88 ms is 1.14x *faster* than evo2's 57.03 ms, while llmlip is
76.00 ms on M5 against 162.32 ms on evo2 (2.14x faster). The ratio moves because
the reference moves, not because our side regresses.

Four measured reasons, in the order they matter:

1. **NEON width and the accumulator traffic.** The dispatched kernel handles 2
   `f64` or 4 `f32` lanes per vector, against 8/16 on AVX-512. Per path and
   vector the per-path form spent five memory operations (three operand loads,
   an accumulator load and a store); at 2 lanes that is 2.5 memory operations
   per element, which is what a single P-core can issue, so the loop ran at the
   load/store limit rather than the multiply limit. §4 removes the two
   accumulator operations; that is the whole library change.
2. **llmlip's VJP kernel reuses operands across the three gradients.** Its
   `mp0b_apply_conv_tp_layer1_scalar_vector_vjp_edge_neon_f64` walks two
   features at a time and updates `bar_x`, `bar_y` and `bar_w` from the same
   `x`, `y`, `w` and `bar` loads, with `bar_weight` register-resident across all
   CG entries. `strided-segpoly`'s descriptor has one output per operation, so
   even after #499's fused traversal it executes six operations (x narrow/wide,
   w narrow/wide, y narrow/wide) and streams the operands once per gradient.
   On AVX-512 the 8/16-wide vectors hide more of that traffic; on 2/4-wide NEON
   they do not. This is the f64 gap (0.718/0.732 vs 0.339/0.381) and the
   remaining f32 gap.
3. **Task fan-out is not the gap.** 1T -> 4T scaling after tuning: f64 fwd
   3.74x/3.54x, f64 VJP 3.77x/3.13x, f32 fwd 3.98x/3.65x, f32 VJP 3.30x/3.28x
   at 128/1024 atoms. Efficiency 0.83-1.00 of linear on four threads; no
   measured reason to change `BLOCK_ROWS` or the split.
4. **Pack and splat are not the gap either.** Profiling one f64 1T VJP cell
   (`sample`, 5 s) puts ~95 % of the strided samples in the dispatched row
   body and ~5 % in `run_task` outside it, which includes the `x1o` lane-stride-3
   pack. The pack is left alone; the splat path is unchanged.

## 4. What changed

### 4.1 An accumulator chunk in registers (`strided-segpoly/src/simd.rs`)

The dispatched row body used to loop paths on the outside and lane vectors on
the inside, calling `axpy_f64`/`axpy_f32` once per path:

```text
for segment:
  for path:
    load acc vector; acc += ((c*a0)*a1)*a2; store acc vector
```

It now splits the segment into chunks of eight SIMD vectors and loops paths
inside a chunk:

```text
for segment:
  for chunk of 8 vectors:
    fetch 8 acc vectors into registers
    for path: add ((c*a0)*a1)*a2 to each of the 8, in path order
    write the 8 back
```

The per-lane statement is exactly the old one: the accumulator starts at its
current value and receives `((c*a0)*a1)*a2` for path 0, then path 1, ..., in
plan order; only the moment it is fetched and written back changes. Products
are still two roundings (no `mul_add`), the vector part and the scalar tail do
the same operations, and the tail is still in increasing lane order. Eight
vectors fit the register file of every level the dispatch can select (NEON,
AVX2, AVX-512), and the scalar level has `S::F64_LANES == 1`, so its "vectors"
are scalars and the same chunking is the scalar fallback. No `target_arch`
case, no width literal.

The remainder (fewer than eight vectors) and the lanes past the last whole
vector take a one-vector form of the same loop, so a short lane axis still
saves its accumulator loads and stores.

### 4.2 Tests (`strided-segpoly/src/simd/tests.rs`)

The mask/arity tests and the segment/path-order test now run 201 lanes instead
of 19. At the widest vector (16 `f32` lanes) that is 12 vectors: one full
eight-vector chunk, a four-vector partial chunk and nine scalar tail lanes, and
every narrower level has a non-empty partial chunk and tail of its own. (A lane
count that is an exact multiple of eight vectors, e.g. 137 at 16 lanes, leaves
`rest` empty and stops testing the partial-chunk path.) Before this change the
chunk path was invisible to the tests on x86 (19 lanes is only two AVX-512 `f64`
vectors). The order test still compares bit patterns against the scalar
statement over two segments and five paths at distinct offsets.

### 4.3 The harness

* `campaign.sh` takes `PIN=auto|0|1` (auto: use `taskset` when present), a
  portable UTC timestamp (BSD `date` has no `-I`), and `SETTLE` seconds before
  each cell and anchor. Defaults preserve the Linux transcript.
* `src/measure.rs` reads `getrusage(RUSAGE_SELF).ru_maxrss` on macOS (bytes
  there, KiB on Linux) so `rss_bytes` is not zero on the M5; Linux still reads
  `/proc/self/status`.
* `raw/llmlip-bench-api.diff` was **malformed as committed by #493**: the two
  new files had no `diff --git`, `new file mode` or `@@ -0,0 +1,N @@` headers,
  and `git apply`/`patch` silently applied only the three modified files, so a
  fresh checkout could not build the `bench-api` feature. It is regenerated
  here with valid headers and applies cleanly to llmlip `6bfb041`.

## 7. Allocation, scratch, numerics and determinism

* **Allocation.** The fused VJP is still 0 bytes / 0 calls per call in every
  cell; the only nonzero `alloc_bytes_total` values are the #496/#499
  1 520-byte rayon worker start-ups on first touch (≤ 0.02 calls/call). Over a
  15-call cell that is 101.3 B/call, which is why the checker's per-call bar is
  200 B (and 0.1 calls/call), not zero: a per-call allocation would show up as
  1 520 B on every call, not once per worker. No new allocation is on the timed
  path.
* **Scratch.** `scratch_len` is unchanged: the chunk is a register pattern, not
  a buffer. The fused plan still needs 2944 elements at budget 1 and 23 552 at
  budget 8, and no `[E,16,128]` array exists; `bar_y`'s accumulator is the
  child's `[16,128]` region.
* **Bit identity.** The per-lane sequence is unchanged, so the crate's
  `a_fused_plan_matches_the_split_plans_bit_for_bit`,
  `a_fused_plan_is_budget_and_thread_invariant` and the scalar-reference tests
  pass; the harness's fused-vs-split comparison passes for both dtypes at 1T
  and 4T. The SIMD order test now covers the chunk, partial chunk and tail with
  non-dyadic payloads and per-path offsets.
* **llmlip agreement** in the accepted after campaign (scale relative, worst of
  the four cells per dtype): f64 forward 6.36e-16, f64 `bar_x` 4.3e-16,
  `bar_w` 5.0e-16, `bar_y` 1.5e-15; f32 forward 2.56e-7, f32 `bar_x` 2.97e-7,
  `bar_w` 2.68e-7, `bar_y` 1.17e-6. These are the same figures #497/#499
  reported; the kernel change did not move the aggregates, and the exact claim
  rests on the bitwise tests.
* **Determinism.** Rows in unit order, operations in descriptor order, paths in
  plan order, chunks and lanes in increasing order, block partials combined
  left to right. Nothing in the chunk loop depends on the number of children.

## 8. Limits

* **No affinity on macOS.** The 4T medians in §5 mix super and performance
  cores; the ratio is stable, the absolute is not. This is why the before/after
  table also gives the four-campaign ratio range and why the cleanest window
  (`after4`) is reported separately.
* **Shape-level, not model-level**, as in #491-#499: synthetic buffers of the
  right shape, llmlip patched locally for its private layer-1 kernel.
* **llmlip's NEON f64 VJP remains structurally faster on this block** (§3.2):
  one pass over three gradients with register accumulators against segpoly's
  one-output-per-operation descriptor. Closing that would be a descriptor
  change (shared operands across outputs), not a lane-width change; it is out
  of scope here and would need its own order/bitwise contract.
* **No SVE/SME path.** Pulp selects NEON on this host; the M5 Max has no SVE.
  The chunking is independent of that choice.
* The x86 campaign was run once on the pinned host, with two repeat transcripts
  in `raw/500-x86-repeats/` (`after1`, `after2`). The tuned `after3` is not
  "within 2 %" of them: across the eight VJP cells the signed difference is
  -5.8 % to +10.0 % against `after1` and -6.9 % to +15.6 % against `after2`.
  `after3` is slower than both repeats on f32 1T 128 (26.95 ms vs 26.62 /
  23.32, +1.2 % / +15.6 %) and on f32 4T 1024 (57.03 vs 51.83 / 52.98,
  +10.0 % / +7.6 %), and faster on f64 1T 128 (43.50 vs 46.18 / 46.73,
  -5.8 % / -6.9 %); the other five cells are within -1.5 % to +2.3 %. The
  forward 4T 1024 cell of `after2` had one outlier sample and is not used.
